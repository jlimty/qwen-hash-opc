# Tiers 2 and 3: a brief worth paying for, credits, and the admin's tools

Date: 22 Nov 2026. Status: draft for the admin's approval. Builds on `tier1-trust-fixes`.

## Intent

The persona review agreed: a bid or no-bid verdict on top of the free price check isn't worth paying for. Tiers 2 and 3 make the paid brief clearly more than the free check. Payment moves from per-brief to credits, so a customer buys ahead and spends as they go.

The admin gets a way to fix a draft rather than decline it, and customers get a profile they can edit, plain-language help and deadline awareness.

**Out of scope:** team accounts and a scheduled feed refresh (the admin skipped both). Email alerts are also out (no email provider). Alerts are in-app only.

## Decisions

### Credits (the admin: S$9.90 a credit; one free credit on sign-up)

- **A credit ledger replaces per-brief payments.**
  - A new table `credit_ledger` holds one row per change: client, delta, reason, request, purchase, time. The balance is the sum.
  - The existing `payments` rows stay as history.
- **Settings decide the price**, stored in `tender_desk.settings`:
  - `credit_price_cents` = `990`
  - `credits_per_brief` = `1`
  - packs `credit_packs` = `5:4950,20:17900,50:39900` (5 for S$49.50, 20 for S$179, 50 for S$399)
  - The admin can reprice without a deploy.
- **The free credit.** Sign-up grants 1 credit, with reason `welcome`.
- **Buying.** `/credits` lists the packs. Buying one in test mode adds the credits and records a `credit_purchases` row with status `test`. The page says "Test mode: no card is charged". The header shows the balance on every signed-in page.
- **Spending.** Asking for a brief debits `credits_per_brief`, in the same transaction that takes the advice slot. With too few credits, checkout shows "Buy credits" instead of the pay button.
- **Refunds are automatic.** A declined or cancelled request credits the same amount back, with reason `refund`. The customer sees it in their credit history.

### A richer brief (code computes every figure; the model writes judgement)

The brief gains four parts. The model's output becomes structured, and code writes the figures.

1. **Suggested price range.**
   - The model picks a band position from a fixed list (`below_lower_quartile`, `lower_quartile_to_median`, `median_to_upper_quartile`, `above_upper_quartile`) and gives its reasons.
   - Code turns the position into figures from the snapshot, for example "S$69,780 to S$90,826", or "below S$21,875" for the lowest band.
   - The price position is no longer free text with no figures.
2. **Who usually wins this work.** Code lists the three most frequent winners among the comparables (how many of the comparable awards each won). If the agency awarded most of them to one supplier, it says so.
3. **Requirements check.**
   - At checkout the customer may paste the tender's key requirements (optional, up to 4000 characters).
   - The model returns up to 8 requirements, each `met`, `partly met` or `gap`, with the verified project that shows it (by title) or what's missing.
   - Without pasted requirements, this section is left out, and checkout says so beforehand.
4. **Before you bid.** Up to 4 questions to ask the agency before the clarification deadline, and up to 3 risks.

All model text keeps today's checks: no digits, no quantities in words, no internal IDs, no price talk outside the price section. Drafting still retries up to three times with the reason fed back.

### Admin: fix a draft without declining it

- **Edit.** The brief review page gets an "Edit" form with the brief text and the structured parts. Saving re-runs every code check (figures, IDs, citations, figures in words) before the change is stored. The edit is audited as "Edited a draft".
- **Redraft with a note.** A short note box ("Mention their school programmes; don't call the scope small") is sent to the model with the facts, and the new draft replaces the pending one.

### Exclusivity

- **Released on a no-bid.** An approved no-bid brief releases the tender, because the client won't bid. The admin confirmed this on 22 Nov, with one condition: **a no-bid brief carries no price advice.** It has no suggested range and no price reasoning, so two competitors can never hold price advice on the same tender. The public quartiles still show, because the free check shows them anyway.
- **Neutral refusal.** The refusal reads "This tender isn't available for a reviewed brief right now." It no longer implies a competitor is bidding, and it links to similar open tenders.

### Customer experience

- **Plain-language help.** One line under the figures explains what median, lower quartile and comparable award mean. The landing page gets a short "How to read this" with ITQ (quotation) and ITT (tender) explained.
- **Profile you can edit.**
  - `/profile` becomes editable after sign-up: name, what you do, categories, team size, capacity, and adding projects.
  - The form starts with three project rows and a note that private-sector projects count. They can be cited once checked, though only government work can be cross-checked against GeBIZ.
  - Saving shows "Profile saved".
- **Sample brief.** A fictional example brief, clearly labelled, is linked from the landing page and checkout, so a customer sees what they get before spending a credit.
- **Deadline awareness.**
  - Home sorts open tenders by closing date, soonest first.
  - Each shows "Closes in N days", in amber when 3 days or fewer.
  - A request page warns when the tender closes before the brief's usual turnaround.
- **Wider coverage.** I'll probe more GeBIZ feed names (facilities, horticulture, pest control, manpower and others) and add every one that returns a real feed, so the cleaning and facilities persona has tenders to see.

## Added 22 Nov: QwenWork as the admin's back office (the admin approved)

At S$9.90 a brief, the admin's review time is the cost. QwenWork takes the preparation; the decisions stay in the console.

- **Operator connector.** A new credential role, `operator`, is issued by the admin from the console and pasted into the admin's own QwenWork. It never approves, declines or verifies anything. Its tools:
  - `list_queue`: waiting requests and pending briefs, soonest-closing first, with risk level.
  - `get_review_pack`: one brief or request with everything needed to judge it: the draft and its parts, the tender, the comparables (outliers flagged), the customer's profile and checked projects, and the risk facts.
  - `add_review_note`: a short note on a brief, with a suggested action (`approve`, `redraft`, `decline`) and an optional redraft note. It's stored, audited and shown in the console.
  - `redraft`: re-runs the draft for a waiting request or a pending brief, with a note, through the same checks.
  - `prepare_project_check`: the GeBIZ cross-check for a project, plus a suggested verdict and evidence, stored as a note on the project.
- **Console.**
  - The agent's notes show on the brief page, the request queue and the project page, labelled "AI reviewer's note (a suggestion; you decide)".
  - The briefs list gets **batch approve**: tick pending briefs and approve them together. Each one still goes through `reviewBrief`, with every check, and gets its own audit row.
  - Only briefs at low or medium risk whose AI note suggests approve can be ticked.
- **Skill.** `skills/tender-desk-operator/SKILL.md` tells the admin's QwenWork how to work the queue. It writes notes, never claims an approval, and points the admin to the console to decide.
